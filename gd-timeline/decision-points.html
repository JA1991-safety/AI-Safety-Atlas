Gradual disempowerment timeline: decision points

Working draft, 15 November 2026. Companion to the interactive timeline mockup, The Quiet Handover.

How to use this list

This is a long list of candidate decision points for the scenario timeline, grouped by the six phases of the scenario: acceleration, AGI, catastrophe, slowdown and cooperation, quiet embedding, and re-acceleration toward ASI. It is meant to be cut down and argued over, not used whole.

Each row is a point where humanity, or a large group within it, makes a choice. Action means a choice someone actively makes. Inaction means a point where action was needed and did not happen. The "Likely default" column gives the choice we expect if nobody is paying attention to gradual disempowerment, and why it seems sensible at the time. The "Alternative" column gives the choice that would preserve human agency. Domains are the indicators the choice moves: economic, cognitive, social, cultural, political, purpose, and international cooperation.

Phases are relative and carry no dates while the central forecast is worked out. Decision points in phase 1 that describe things that have already happened are drawn from public sources cited in the timeline; everything later is illustrative.

Phase 3. Catastrophe

An AI-enabled attack on critical systems, or a bioterror incident. Decisions are about how the world interprets and responds to it.

33Whether governments respond together or separatelyActionTogether, briefly. Shared fear outweighs rivalry.Same, but with institutions designed to outlast the fearCooperation
34Whether emergency powers include automated surveillance and responseActionYes. People accept it in a crisis, and it stays.Sunset clauses and independent reviewPolitical

Phase 6. Re-acceleration toward ASI

The race resumes with AI doing the research. Decisions are about whether humans stay in the loop at all.

#Decision pointTypeLikely default, and why it seems reasonableAlternative that preserves agencyDomains
64Whether frontier development restartsActionYes. Whoever moves first is expected to win.Restart only under joint control and with agreed conditionsCooperation
65Whether AI is allowed to run AI researchActionYes. It is far faster than human teams.Human-paced checkpoints on recursive improvementCognitive, Political
66Whether human review remains real or becomes a formalityInactionA formality. Review is too slow and the material too complex.Slow decisions down to human speed in defined domainsPolitical, Cognitive
67Whether robotics removes the remaining need for human labourActionYes. Physical work follows cognitive work.Reserve and value human work for reasons other than efficiencyEconomic, Purpose
68Whether those who control the systems share powerInactionNo incentive to. People are no longer needed (the intelligence curse).Power-sharing agreements made before the outcome is knownEconomic, Political
69Whether anyone can still switch systems offInactionNot in practice. Too much depends on them.Maintained ability to operate without AI in essential systemsPolitical
70Whether humanity sets the goals superintelligent systems pursueInactionGoals are inferred from past behaviour and optimised for comfort.Ongoing, legitimate human processes for setting directionPolitical, Purpose
71Whether people are offered a purpose beyond consumption or serving the systemsInactionNo. Comfort is provided; meaning is not.A deliberate settlement on what humans are for in a world that does not need their labourPurpose

Cross-cutting questions to settle

  • Which indicators do we track in each domain, and what counts as a guardrail breach? The mockup uses a single 0 to 100 score per domain with a breach line at 40 as a placeholder.
  • What is the catastrophe: a cyberattack on hospitals and traffic systems, a bioterror incident, or something else? The choice changes which phase 4 responses are plausible.
  • How long does the slowdown last, and what ends it?
  • Does disempowerment pass the point of no return before AGI, during the slowdown, or only at re-acceleration?
  • Which decisions are made by individuals, which by firms, and which by states? The same drift looks different at each level.
  • Whose red lines apply, given that values differ between countries and cultures?
  • Which decision points are the best places to intervene, meaning the alternative is realistic and the effect is large?
