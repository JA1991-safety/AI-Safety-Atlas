# Editorial charter

*Draft for the editor to approve.*

## Purpose

Common Ground helps people understand how AI safety is seen, governed and argued about in different countries, so that countries can work together on it. Its readers are safety researchers and policymakers, and anyone else who wants to understand another country's position before judging it.

## The one position we take

We take no side between countries, governments, parties or companies. The only position the project takes is that cooperation on AI safety is worth working for, and that understanding comes before agreement.

## Source policy

1. **No source, no claim.** Every statement links to evidence, with a publication date and labels for origin, type and language.
2. **Primary sources first.** The law, the poll release, the official statement or the speech, ahead of reporting about them.
3. **Culture and industry: inside voices first.** What a country's people, officials, researchers, companies, unions and media say about themselves is the main evidence. At least 60% of a culture section's sources come from inside the country, or the section says plainly that it falls short.
4. **Facts: inside and outside weighted equally.** For laws, institutions, dates and figures, a source's origin doesn't change its weight.
5. **Polls are reported with their method.** Fieldwork dates, sample size and the question as asked, wherever available.
6. **Gaps are stated, not filled.** A short section reads as a gap in our evidence, not as a lack of activity in the country.

## What we publish

- Descriptions of what governments, companies and organisations did and said, in neutral language
- Disagreement inside a country, presented as disagreement
- "Why it matters for AI safety" notes that explain the link to safety, not whether a development is good or bad
- Quotes in the speaker's own words and original language, with a translation beside them

## What we don't publish

- Rankings, scores or league tables of countries
- Characterisations of motive ("cynical", "genuine") or judgement words ("lagging", "draconian", "world-leading") in our own voice. Such words may appear inside a quote, attributed to the speaker.
- Positions on disputed territory. Borders are shown for navigation only.
- Anything that could put a contributor at risk

## The same standard for every country

Each country gets the same structure and the same level of detail where evidence allows. Before publishing a sentence, we apply the **swap test**: would it read as fair if it were written about a different country?

## Review and accountability

- Nothing is published without the editor's approval. Culture sections also need a reviewer from the country. Sensitive topics need a second reviewer.
- Every page has a "suggest a correction" link. Corrections are logged publicly.
- Anyone described on the site has a right of reply.
- Contributors declare their interests, and the project declares its own: drafts are prepared with Claude, made by Anthropic, so anything mentioning Anthropic or its models gets a second reviewer.
- The editor has the final say, and each decision to overrule a reviewer is noted in the pull request.
